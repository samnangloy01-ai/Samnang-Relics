Prasat Pram is an impressive 10th-century temple located within the Koh Ker archaeological landscape (situated in Preah Vihear province, near the border of Siem Reap). Historical Background: The temple was built during the 10th century (between 921 and 941 AD) during the reign of King Jayavarman IV, when he established his capital at Koh Ker (known historically as Chok Gargyar) for the Khmer Empire. Architecture: Despite its name "Prasat Pram" (meaning "Five Temples"), the site actually consists of six brick and sandstone towers standing on a single laterite platform, facing eastward. Nature and Heritage: The most iconic feature of Prasat Pram is one of its brick sanctuary towers being entirely embraced and entwined by massive, ancient tree roots. This breathtaking fusion of ancient brickwork and thriving nature creates a mystical atmosphere reminiscent of the famous temples in Angkor, making it a favorite spot for travelers and photographers.

Located on the remote border region between Siem Reap and Preah Vihear, featuring classic architectural elements of the period.

Prasat Pram is an impressive 10th-century temple located within the UNESCO World Heritage site of Koh Ker, roughly a 1.5 to 2-hour drive from Siem Reap. History and Era: The temple was built during the 10th century (between 921 and 941 AD) during the reign of King Jayavarman IV when Koh Ker served as the capital of the Khmer Empire. Architectural Structure: Despite its name meaning "Five Temples," the site actually consists of five main towers standing on a single laterite and sandstone base: The Three Brick Towers (Back Row): Aligned from north to south, facing east, and originally dedicated to Lord Shiva. The Front Structures: Includes structures identified as a library and a firehouse built from laterite, facing west. Nature's Embrace: The most iconic feature of Prasat Pram is how massive, ancient tree roots have tightly grown over and intertwined with the brick towers. This stunning combination of ancient human architecture and thriving nature creates a breathtaking view for visitors and photographers.

A remarkable brick temple ruin located within the majestic archaeological complex of Koh Ker.
